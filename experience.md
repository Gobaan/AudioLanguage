# User Experience — Learning Journey Over Time

## First Time User

They open the app, pick their language. No tutorial. No word list. No settings screen.

They're dropped straight into a scene — a simple image of two people meeting. A short audio clip plays: two voices, a greeting exchange, natural speed. They hear it twice. A prompt appears: *"The person on the right is greeting you. What do they say?"* — multiple choice, four options, all audio. They pick one, hear it played back.

Then: *"Now you try. Greet them back."* A speaking prompt. They attempt it. The app plays the correct version and lets them compare. No judgement — just exposure and attempt.

Three or four exchanges like this. The whole scene takes 5 minutes. At the end: *"You can now greet someone in Sinhala. Want to try it with a real conversation?"* — the greeting chatbot scenario unlocks. They spend 2 minutes talking to an AI neighbour who greets them and asks their name. It's clunky and slow, but it works. They said something in a new language on day one.

Total first session: 15 minutes. They leave knowing 8-10 phrases, having had their first real exchange.

---

## 1 Day User

They come back. The app shows them: *"3 scenes to review, 2 new scenes today."* Their session is:

- Two new scenes: buying water, saying thank you
- Three review scenes from yesterday at different retrieval challenges — one where they hear and identify, one where they see the image and must produce, one where they hear only the response and must say what prompted it
- Five minutes in the greeting chatbot — the AI now asks where they're from, adds one new phrase to the exchange

Session: 20 minutes. They now have ~25 phrases. The market scenario is half-unlocked — they can see it greyed out with a progress bar.

---

## 1 Week User

They've built a habit. They open the app knowing roughly what to expect. Their session is:

- One or two new scenes per day — the rate has slowed slightly because review is taking more time as the library grows
- A mix of retrieval challenges on ~15 scenes from the past week, each at a different difficulty based on how they performed last time
- They've unlocked the market scenario and the asking-for-directions scenario in the chatbot
- The market conversation is getting smoother — they've done it four times and can now handle "how much / too expensive / I'll take it" without freezing

They have ~100 words and phrases. They've started noticing the same words appear across different scenes. *"That word means 'want' — I've heard it in three different scenes now."* The slots are forming without being taught.

Session: 20-25 minutes. The grind is low because every review has a payoff — a scene they can place — and the chatbot sessions feel like achievement rather than drilling.

---

## 1 Month User

They have ~400-500 phrases and words. The scenes are getting harder — the market scene now has a difficult customer who haggles, the directions scene has a confusing intersection. New scenes are introducing more complex language — time, numbers, family.

Their session looks like:

- 5 new scene exposures
- 10-15 scene reviews, mostly at production level now — hearing the scene and having to produce the response, not just identify it
- One full chatbot conversation, 10-15 minutes, with a scenario they chose: they're trying the doctor's visit for the first time. It's hard. They get stuck twice. The AI offers a hint. They finish it feeling like they actually accomplished something.
- At the end of the session the app shows them: *"You struggled with body vocabulary today. Three body-part scenes added to this week's queue."*

Session: 30 minutes. The feedback loop is visible — weaknesses get targeted, strengths get maintained with less time.

---

## 1 Year User

They have 2000+ words. They've been through most of the scene library. A meaningful percentage of their vocabulary has crossed the 30+ day spaced repetition threshold — genuinely retained, not just recently drilled.

Their session barely resembles the first-time user experience:

- Almost no new scenes — maybe one a week introducing edge vocabulary
- Light spaced repetition review of the deepest vocabulary — maybe 5 minutes of cards for words that are due
- The majority of their session is a 20-30 minute AI conversation in a complex scenario — a job interview, a phone call with a relative, navigating a bureaucratic situation
- The chatbot is no longer slow and patient — it's set to natural speed, uses colloquial language, occasionally doesn't repeat things
- They're starting to notice when the AI makes unnatural phrasing — they know the language well enough to feel when something is off

Their relationship with the app has fundamentally shifted. It started as a teacher. It's now a practice partner. The scenes are mostly done. The conversation layer is where the growth is happening.

If they ever encounter a word in conversation they don't know, they can flag it — and a scene will be created around it for their next review session.

---

## The Arc

**Scene library → conversation partner → maintenance tool.**

Most apps never get past step one. This one is designed so step two starts on day one and step three begins at month six.
