# Character Bible

## Global Style

Simple flat webcomic illustration for language-learning cards. Use clean dark line art, warm flat colors, expressive human faces, readable gestures, and simple real-world backgrounds. Keep rendering clear at phone size.

Avoid photorealism, mascots, animal characters, dramatic lighting, heavy texture, captions, subtitles, translations, logos, and readable dialogue text.

## Learner Avatar

The learner is the user's recurring visual stand-in. Use `visuals/style/characters/learner-reference.png` as the approved identity reference.

- young adult woman, friendly and approachable
- medium build, average height
- short dark wavy hair
- warm medium skin tone
- teal zip hoodie over a plain white shirt
- dark navy pants
- simple gray sneakers
- small tan crossbody bag used as a recurring prop
- expressive but not exaggerated face

The learner should feel curious, slightly nervous in new situations, and capable. They should be easy to recognize from scene to scene.

## Partner Archetypes

Create reusable partner reference sheets as needed:

- staff: calm counter worker for offices, transport desks, schools, and service counters
- vendor: friendly food or shop worker
- friend: casual peer who greets and responds warmly
- pharmacist: professional pharmacy worker with a clean counter setting
- local helper: ordinary passerby who can give directions

Partner archetypes should be human and context-appropriate. Do not create a new partner design when an existing archetype can serve the role.

## Turn-Taking Conventions

- Cue panel: partner is visually active; learner is listening.
- Learner-turn panel: learner is visually active and easy to identify.
- Resolution panel: partner reacts; the result of the learner's response is visible.
- Prefer stable medium comic panels with both characters readable.
- Keep the learner in similar clothing and palette across all cards.

## Speech and Meaning Bubbles

Speech bubbles may be empty or contain only `...` to show speaking turns. Do not put target dialogue, translations, or phonetics inside generated images.

Use symbolic meaning bubbles for hidden or specific meaning:

- pain location, cough, fever, stomach trouble
- lost passport, wallet, phone
- desired food, medicine, water
- map pin, clock, form confusion

Use body cues for visible social meaning:

- wave for greeting
- small bow or raised hand for apology/attention
- puzzled face for confusion
- pointing or holding object for requests
- map and gaze direction for directions

When useful, combine both. For a leg-pain card, show the learner favoring one leg and add a small leg icon with pain rays.

## Identity Invariants

The learner should always keep:

- same female learner identity as `learner-reference.png`
- short dark wavy hair
- teal hoodie and white shirt
- dark navy pants
- tan crossbody bag
- same simple webcomic face proportions
- same warm flat-color style

Small pose, expression, and lighting changes are fine. Do not change age, body type, outfit colors, hairstyle, or species.

## Review Checklist

A generated image is not approved if:

- the learner looks like a different person
- the learner outfit or bag changes substantially
- the image includes readable target text, subtitles, or translations
- the scene is decorative but does not clarify the learning moment
- symbolic bubbles use English labels instead of icons
- body language is too vague to infer the learner's intention
